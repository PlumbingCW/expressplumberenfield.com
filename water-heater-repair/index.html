When a water heater problem is an emergency

Three situations need a fast response: water leaking from the tank body, a gas smell near the heater, and a relief valve that keeps discharging. Anything else, such as lukewarm water or a noisy tank, is a repair we can schedule.

Gas comes first. If you smell gas, leave the building and call your gas utility or 911 before you call a plumber.

  1. Shut the cold water supply valve on the pipe above the tank.
  2. Turn an electric unit off at the breaker. Turn a gas unit's control to off or pilot.
  3. If you smell gas, do not touch switches. Leave the house and call your gas utility or 911 from outside.
  4. Put a bucket or towels under a slow drip and move stored items off the floor.
  5. Call (860) 397-8103 and tell dispatch the fuel, the age and where the water is coming from.

Gas, electric or hybrid: what usually fails

On a gas tank, the common faults are a dirty or failing thermocouple, a pilot that will not stay lit, a gas control valve that has worn out and a burner that sooted up. On an electric tank, the heating elements and thermostats fail, and a tripped high-limit switch looks like total failure when it is a simple reset.

Hybrid heat pump models add a compressor, a fan and a condensate drain to the list, which is why a hybrid that stops heating is worth a proper diagnosis before anyone orders parts.

No hot water in November is more than an inconvenience. Call and we will sort repair from replacement over the phone.

Leaks: where the water comes from decides everything

Water at the base of the tank is not always the tank. A loose supply fitting, a dripping relief valve discharge pipe, a failed drain valve and condensation on a high-efficiency unit all put water on the floor. We trace it to the source before we say the word replacement.

If the water is coming through a seam in the tank itself, the steel has failed and no part will fix it. At that point the honest recommendation is water heater replacement, and we explain why with the evidence in front of you.

Safety devices and settings

Water heater safety devices such as the temperature and pressure relief valve are required by the 2021 International Plumbing Code (IPC), which the 2022 Connecticut State Building Code adopts. The US Department of Energy suggests a 120 degree setting for most homes, which also lowers scald risk. The Connecticut Department of Consumer Protection licenses plumbers, so you can ask any plumber about their license before work starts.
