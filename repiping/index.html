Whole-House Repiping in Enfield, CT

Repiping in Enfield, CT means replacing the water supply pipes in a house when the old ones have worn out. The signs are usually low pressure, rusty or discolored water and leaks that come back after each repair. It is a planned job, and we explain the options and the scope before anyone opens a wall.

If a pipe has burst, shut the main valve and call dispatch for burst pipe repair, then plan the repipe afterward.

Signs your house may need repiping

A single leak does not mean the whole house needs new pipe. A pattern does. Pressure that drops when two fixtures run, brown or orange water at the tap, pinhole leaks in more than one place and fittings that crumble when touched all point to supply pipe that is reaching the end of its life.

Galvanized steel is the usual culprit in older homes, because it corrodes from the inside and narrows the bore until flow drops. Pipes with repeated leaks get a leak detection visit first, so we know whether the problem is the whole system or one section.

Repipe, repair or replace in stages

Match the response to what the pipes show.

  • <strong>One leak in a good system.</strong> Repair the section and move on.
  • <strong>Repeated leaks in the same material.</strong> The pipe is probably worn throughout, and a repipe is worth pricing.
  • <strong>Low pressure and rusty water.</strong> Galvanized supply is the likely cause. Replacement restores flow.
  • <strong>Planned remodel.</strong> If walls are already open, repiping that section is cheaper than doing it later.
  • <strong>Mixed materials.</strong> A house with copper, galvanized and plastic may be repiped in stages.

Not every old house needs a repipe. Call and we will tell you what the pipes actually show.

Copper or PEX: how they compare

Both are acceptable choices when installed correctly. Each has trade-offs.

FactorCopperPEX
InstallationSoldered joints, more laborFlexible tubing, fewer fittings, faster
Material costHigherLower
Freeze resistanceSplits when frozenStretches, often survives a freeze
Sun exposureUnaffectedMust be kept out of sunlight
LongevityMany decades with good waterLong track record, newer than copper

How a repipe is done

We start with a plan. We locate the main shutoff, map each fixture and decide where the new lines run through basements, crawlspaces and walls. Small access holes in drywall let us pull PEX through without tearing out whole rooms, and we patch what we open or leave it ready for the finish trade.

Water is off for part of the day, and we schedule around that. After the new lines are in, we pressure test them, flush the system and check each fixture. We confirm what permits the Enfield building office needs for your work.

Planning the work around daily life

A repipe disrupts a household less than people fear, but it helps to plan. We shut the water off for a set window each day, tell you which rooms we will work in and keep the main fixture, usually one bathroom or the kitchen sink, usable at the end of each day where we can. Pets and children should be kept out of the work area.

Before we start, move furniture and stored items away from walls we will open, and tell us about any fixtures with special connections, such as an icemaker or a water filter. A short walkthrough with the crew lead on day one catches most surprises.

Permits and code for repiping

A whole-house repipe is a regulated project under the 2021 International Plumbing Code (IPC), which the 2022 Connecticut State Building Code adopts. Whether a permit is needed depends on the job and the town, and the Enfield building department can confirm it. A permit and inspection protect you at resale, because the work is on record with the town.

Enfield has houses from many eras, including the older homes along Enfield Street in the Enfield Historic District, and a house of any age may have had piping replaced in stages. Where one material meets another, the joint is often the weak link, so we identify each material on site.
