What a good installation includes

A water heater that is installed well runs for years without a callback. That means correct gas line sizing or electrical supply, a vent that draws properly, a relief valve with a proper discharge pipe, new flexible connectors and shutoff valves you can actually reach. It also means a drain pan where the unit sits over finished space.

We test every connection under pressure, check combustion on gas units and show you the shutoff valve, the thermostat and the relief valve before we leave.

Choosing your new unit

These are the decisions that matter before you buy.

  • <strong>Fuel.</strong> Gas, electric and propane are all possible, and your house's existing supply usually decides it.
  • <strong>Size.</strong> Pick the tank by household size and peak use, not by the old tank's label.
  • <strong>Type.</strong> Standard tank, hybrid heat pump or tankless each trade cost, space and efficiency differently.
  • <strong>Venting.</strong> A chimney that served an older heater may not be right for a high-efficiency one.
  • <strong>Warranty.</strong> Compare tank warranty length and what labor it covers.

Installing before the old tank fails costs less than replacing it after a flood. Call and we will book a visit.

Gas, electric and venting details

Gas units must be vented to the outside, and the right method depends on the model. A natural draft unit uses a chimney or a liner, while a power vent or direct vent unit uses a fan and plastic pipe through a wall. A chimney that is too large can let flue gases cool and spill back, so older chimneys sometimes need a liner.

Electric units need a dedicated circuit sized for the elements. A hybrid heat pump unit needs space around it and a drain for the condensate. We verify each of these on the site visit instead of discovering them on installation day.

Code rules for a new installation

A new water heater installation must meet the 2021 International Plumbing Code (IPC), which the 2022 Connecticut State Building Code adopts, covering the relief valve, discharge piping and shutoff. Whether a permit is needed depends on the job and the town, and the Enfield building department can confirm it. The Connecticut Department of Consumer Protection licenses plumbers, so you can ask any plumber about their license before work starts.
