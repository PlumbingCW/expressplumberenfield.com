No heat or a boiler leak: what to do first

A boiler that is leaking or has lost heat in cold weather puts both the house and the pipes at risk. Pipes freeze quickly in a house with no heat, so speed matters.

Gas comes first. If you smell gas, leave the building and call your gas utility or 911 before you call a plumber.

  1. Check the thermostat, the boiler's power switch and the fuel supply.
  2. If water is leaking from the boiler, switch it off at the service switch and close the water feed valve.
  3. Do not remove the relief valve or cap its discharge.
  4. Open cabinet doors on exterior walls and let a trickle run at a faucet on a very cold night.
  5. Call (860) 397-8103 and say what you see and hear.

What we handle on a boiler call

We work on the water side of a hot water boiler: leaks at fittings and valves, the circulator pump, the expansion tank, the pressure relief valve, the fill valve, zone valves and the diverter or tee fittings on a hydronic loop. A boiler that cycles, bangs or loses pressure is usually telling you something about one of these.

Burner and combustion faults are a different part of the system, and we confirm on the call whether the problem you describe is something we service before we send a crew.

A boiler problem in a Connecticut winter cannot wait. Call and we will tell you what to check while we head over.

Diverter valves and one-pipe systems

Older steam and one-pipe systems use diverter tees and air vents that wear out. A stuck diverter sends water to the wrong place and leaves some radiators cold. Replacing it is a plumbing job and often a quick one when the shutoffs are good.

We also drain and refill the system carefully so air does not get back in, because trapped air is the main reason a repaired loop still heats poorly.

Safety rules for boilers

Boiler repair involves gas, combustion and pressure, so work should be done by a licensed professional under local code. Water side piping follows the 2021 International Plumbing Code (IPC), which the 2022 Connecticut State Building Code adopts. If you smell gas, leave the building and call your gas utility or 911 first. The Connecticut Department of Consumer Protection licenses plumbers, so you can ask any plumber about their license before work starts.
