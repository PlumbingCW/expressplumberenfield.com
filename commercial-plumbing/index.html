When a commercial plumbing problem is an emergency

A sewer backup in a restaurant, a burst line over inventory or a failed water heater in a salon can shut a business down. The first step is to stop the damage and protect the building.

Use these steps while the crew is on the way.

  1. Stop using sinks, toilets and floor drains if the line is backing up.
  2. Close the main water valve for a burst line, and open a low faucet to drain the pipes.
  3. Move stock, paper goods and electronics off the floor.
  4. Photograph the problem for your insurer and your landlord.
  5. Call (860) 397-8103 and say who can give access if the owner is not on site.

What commercial service covers

Commercial buildings have longer drain runs, larger pipes and heavier use than houses. The common calls are main-line backups, grease-laden kitchen drains, failed water heaters, leaking supply lines, broken fixtures and frozen pipes in unheated spaces. We handle these with the same camera, cable and jetting equipment as residential work, scaled to the pipe.

For a restaurant or a shop with a recurring backup, hydro jetting and a camera inspection find the real cause instead of clearing the same clog each month.

A business with a plumbing problem needs a time, not a maybe. Call and dispatch will give you the plan.

Grease traps, backflow testing and storm drains

Food service kitchens usually need a grease trap or interceptor, and cleaning it on schedule is the cheapest way to keep a kitchen line open. A trap that is skipped overflows or sends grease downstream into the main. We service traps and set a cleaning interval based on what the kitchen produces.

Backflow preventers on commercial water lines have to be tested on a schedule set by your water supplier or the town, and we confirm what applies to your property. Private storm drains and catch basins in parking lots also clog with leaves and silt, and we clear and flush them.

Preventive visits that keep a business open

The cheapest commercial plumbing call is the one that never happens. A short seasonal visit to check shutoff valves, water heaters, floor drains and backflow devices catches worn parts before they fail on a busy Saturday. Kitchens benefit from a set grease trap and drain-jetting schedule, and offices benefit from a list of shutoff locations posted near the mechanical room.

Ask dispatch about a maintenance plan, and keep a short sheet by the phone with the main shutoff, the water heater location and the building contact, so a night crew can start without delay.

Code and compliance for commercial work

Commercial plumbing follows the Connecticut State Building Code, which adopts the 2021 International Plumbing Code (IPC), which the 2022 Connecticut State Building Code adopts. Backflow devices, grease control and fixture counts are code items, and the building inspector has the final word on a given space. The Connecticut Department of Consumer Protection licenses plumbers, so you can ask any plumber about their license before work starts.

What to do before help arrives

If a commercial space has a plumbing emergency, these steps protect people and property while you wait.

  • Shut off the main water valve if you can reach it safely
  • Turn off power to wet areas if water is near outlets, panels or equipment
  • Move stock, records and electronics out of the water path
  • Keep customers and staff away from sewage or standing water
  • Take photos of the damage for your insurance claim, then call us
