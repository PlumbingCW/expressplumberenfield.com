Where Thompsonville sits and why it matters for a plumber

Thompsonville lies on the east bank of the Connecticut River in the town of Enfield, with Interstate 91 along its eastern edge. Route 5, also called Enfield Street, is its main road, and Route 190 crosses it. The area grew around a carpet mill that Orrin Thompson opened in 1829, and carpet production continued until 1971.

For a service call, the layout matters more than the history. Streets here are closer together than in the outer parts of town, and many buildings sit close to the sidewalk. We plan for street parking and tell you when we are on the way.

What is documented about housing in Thompsonville

The 2020 census counted 3,792 housing units and 3,491 households in the Thompsonville census-designated place, with a median age of 34.7 among its 8,514 residents. Reference sources describe brick houses in the area and Victorian houses on Pearl Street.

The former Bigelow-Sanford carpet works is now an apartment complex called Bigelow Commons, and it is listed on the National Register of Historic Places. We have no records on pipe materials or building ages here, so we confirm what a building has when we open it up.

Living in a building with several units? Before you call

A leak or a backup in a shared building has an owner, and it helps to know whose problem it is before the plumber arrives.

  • Find the shutoff for your own unit and close it if water is coming from a fixture or supply line.
  • Tell your landlord or building manager at the same time you call, since they control the main shutoff.
  • Note which rooms below or beside you are affected and take photos of the water or stain.
  • Check whether neighboring units have the same slow drain, because that points to a shared line.
  • Keep the path to the bathroom, kitchen or basement clear for the plumber.

Downtown storefronts and small businesses

Downtown Thompsonville has storefronts and small businesses alongside housing, so a plumbing call can be a restaurant drain, a restroom fixture or a water heater in a back room. A business has to keep working, and we ask what is still usable before we start.

If the problem touches a building you do not own, tell us who the owner or manager is. We can explain what we find, but the owner decides on the repair.

Code and permits in shared buildings

Plumbing work in a multi-unit building still follows the 2021 International Plumbing Code (IPC), which the 2022 Connecticut State Building Code adopts. Tell your landlord before work starts, and ask the Enfield building department whether a permit applies.
