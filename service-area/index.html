What each town is like for plumbing

Suffield is the farthest west of the river towns, with older homes around its center and a mix of public water, private wells and septic systems. Our Suffield page covers what that mix means when a pipe fails or a drain backs up, and how we plan around a well or a septic tank.

Windsor Locks is a river and canal town with close-built older housing, multi-family houses and businesses near Bradley International Airport. Our Windsor Locks page explains how shared drain lines and after-hours business calls change the plan.

East Windsor, Somers and Ellington

East Windsor has two distinct villages, Warehouse Point and Broad Brook, each with older housing, and basements near the river that need a working sump pump. The East Windsor page covers water heaters, leaks and flooded basements for both ZIP codes.

Somers and Ellington sit in Tolland County. Somers is rural, with many wells and septic systems, and the Somers page explains how that changes a service call. Ellington has more recent suburban housing, and the Ellington page focuses on water heaters, fixtures and finished basements.

Roads, bridges and how a crew reaches each town

Dispatch thinks in routes. To reach Windsor Locks, a crew crosses the Connecticut River on the Route 140 bridge. To reach Suffield, the usual way is the Route 190 and Route 75 corridor. East Windsor is a run south along I-91 and Route 5, and Ellington and Somers are reached east and southeast along Route 83.

Weather, bridge traffic and rush hour change those times, so we give an honest estimate when you call and update you if something changes. A call that comes in during a storm is the one where a crew is also busy elsewhere, and we say so plainly instead of promising a time we cannot keep.

Planned work in nearby towns

Service away from Enfield is not only for emergencies. Homeowners in the surrounding towns book water heater installations, sewer camera inspections, repiping and new bathrooms with us as scheduled visits, and we plan those around the day and the route. A planned visit is also the best time to ask about your shutoff valves, your cleanout location and any pipe you are unsure about.

If you are buying a house in any of these towns, a sewer camera inspection before closing is worth booking. It shows the pipe from the cleanout toward the street, and it gives you a recording to share with the seller or your agent.

Before you call from outside Enfield

A few details speed up a call from any of these towns. Give dispatch your street and village, say whether you are on public water or a well, and tell us if the building is a house, a rental or a business. If you are on a well, switch off the pump breaker when you close the main shutoff.

If the problem is a leak, close the main shutoff and drain the lowest faucet. If the problem is a backup, stop using water entirely. Then call, and dispatch plans the visit while you are still on the phone.

How we decide where to go

We dispatch by driving distance, not by a circle on a map. A town across the river may be closer on a map and farther by road. We give honest arrival estimates on the call, and we tell you if a call is outside what we can reach quickly.

Just across the state line, Longmeadow and East Longmeadow, Massachusetts, are close to Enfield. Tell dispatch your address and we confirm whether we can serve it.

One code across every town

All five towns we serve follow the same state code, which adopts the 2021 International Plumbing Code (IPC), which the 2022 Connecticut State Building Code adopts. Each town building department handles its own permits.
