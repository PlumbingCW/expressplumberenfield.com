Tankless Water Heater Installation, Repair and Flushing in Enfield, CT

A tankless water heater in Enfield, CT heats water as you use it instead of keeping a tank hot all day. It can free up floor space and give a steady supply, but it is a larger change than swapping one tank for another, and it only suits some houses.

We install, repair and flush tankless units, and we tell you plainly when a standard tank would serve you better.

How a tankless unit differs from a tank

A tank heater stores a set volume and reheats it as it cools. A tankless unit fires a burner or powers elements only when water flows, so it never runs out while it is working, but it has a maximum flow rate. Run a shower, a dishwasher and a laundry load together and a small unit may fall short.

Because tankless units need a lot of fuel or power in a short burst, a gas unit often needs a larger gas line and a new vent. An electric tankless unit can need a heavy circuit that the panel does not have to spare.

Is tankless a fit for your house

Answer these before you decide.

  • <strong>Gas supply.</strong> Is the gas line large enough, and is the meter sized for the added load?
  • <strong>Venting.</strong> Is there a route for new vent pipe to the outside?
  • <strong>Electrical capacity.</strong> For electric models, does the panel have room for the circuit?
  • <strong>Water quality.</strong> Sediment and scale build up in the heat exchanger, so ask your water supplier about its report.
  • <strong>Demand.</strong> How many fixtures run at the same time in your home?

A tankless unit is a good fit for some houses and a poor one for others. Call and we will tell you which yours is.

Flushing and descaling: the maintenance that matters

Minerals in the water coat the heat exchanger inside a tankless unit. The coating cuts efficiency, causes error codes and shortens the unit's life. A flush with a descaling solution cleans it out. The right interval depends on your water, so we do not promise a number. Ask your supplier for its water quality report and we will set a schedule from the unit's manual.

Flushing also lets us check the inlet filter, the condensate drain on a condensing model and the burner or elements. Skipping it is how a good unit ends up as a repair call.

Code, venting and gas supply

A tankless install follows the 2021 International Plumbing Code (IPC), which the 2022 Connecticut State Building Code adopts for the water side, and gas sizing and venting follow the manufacturer instructions and local code. Whether a permit is needed depends on the job and the town, and the Enfield building department can confirm it.
