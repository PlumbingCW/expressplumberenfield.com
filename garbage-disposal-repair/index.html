When a garbage disposal problem is an emergency

A leak from the body of a disposal, or a sink full of backed-up water with a disposal that will not drain, is the urgent version of this repair. Never put a hand into a disposal, even when it is off.

Take these steps while the crew is on the way.

  1. Switch off the disposal and turn off its breaker if you can reach the panel safely.
  2. Close the sink's hot and cold supply valves under the cabinet.
  3. Do not run the dishwasher if it connects to the disposal.
  4. Put a bucket under the leak and move cleaning supplies out of the cabinet.
  5. Call (860) 397-8103 and say where the water appears and what you hear when it runs.

Jammed, humming or dead: what each one means

A disposal that hums and does not spin is jammed. The impeller is stuck on something, and a hex key in the bottom of the unit can free it. One that does nothing at all, with no hum, has probably tripped its reset button or lost power. A unit that runs but leaks at the sides has a failed seal or a cracked housing, and no part will fix a cracked housing.

A disposal that spins but the sink still drains slowly points to the drain behind it. When that happens, drain cleaning may be the real fix.

A disposal that leaks under the sink damages the cabinet floor quickly. Call and we will tell you how to hold it.

Replacing or installing a disposal

A new disposal is mounted on the sink flange and connected to the drain trap and, if there is one, the dishwasher hose. The unit needs a nearby outlet or a wired connection and a switch. We check the power, the mounting and the plumbing before we pick the unit, so the new one fits the cabinet.

Dishwasher connections need the knockout plug removed on the new disposal, which is easy to forget and causes a dishwasher backup. We check it and test the whole sink before we leave.
