When a clogged drain is an emergency

One slow bathroom sink can wait until morning. Water rising in a basement floor drain, a toilet that bubbles when the washing machine drains, or two fixtures backing up at once points to a blockage in the main line, and that should not wait.

Until the crew arrives, these steps limit the damage and make the diagnosis faster.

  1. Stop using sinks, showers, the dishwasher and the washing machine.
  2. Skip chemical drain cleaner on a standing backup. It sits on the blockage and burns whoever opens the pipe next.
  3. Photograph wet floors and belongings now, because your insurance adjuster will ask for them.
  4. Note which fixtures are slow or gurgling. That pattern tells us whether the clog is in a branch or the main.
  5. Call (860) 397-8103 and describe the pattern to dispatch.

Why drains clog in older Enfield homes

Thompsonville and the Hazardville Historic District include some homes from the 1800s alongside newer ones, and a drain line in any older house may have been repaired or extended more than once. Where materials or joints change, grease and hair can catch and roots can find a gap. When the same drain blocks twice, the pipe is often the cause, not the household.

That is why a second blockage on the same line is our cue to recommend a sewer camera inspection before anyone pays for a third cable run.

A drain that is already overflowing is the wrong time to compare quotes. Dispatch answers all night and weekend calls.

Snaking, cabling and when we step up to jetting

Small fixture lines get a cable with a cutting head sized to the pipe. A 1-1/2 inch kitchen trap arm takes a lighter cable than a 4 inch main, and using the wrong size either skips the clog or scars the pipe. We run the cable until the blockage breaks or is retrieved, then flush with water to confirm flow.

A cable can punch a hole through grease and leave the walls coated, so the drain slows again in weeks. In that case hydro jetting scours the full pipe wall, and sewer line cleaning covers the main out to the street.

Plumbing code and drain work

Drain and trap work in Connecticut is governed by the 2021 International Plumbing Code (IPC), which the 2022 Connecticut State Building Code adopts. That code sets how traps and vents must be arranged, which is why a slow drain is sometimes a venting problem rather than a clog. The Connecticut Department of Consumer Protection licenses plumbers, so you can ask any plumber about their license before work starts.

Drains, cleanouts and the town sewer in Enfield

The Town of Enfield runs its own Water Pollution Control Facility, and properties on the town sewer have a lateral between the house and the street main. Homes on septic have different failure points. Before any work we find your cleanout so we know which side of the connection the problem sits on.
