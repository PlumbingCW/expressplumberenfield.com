Wells, septic tanks and what they change

A rural house is a small utility of its own. A well pump and pressure tank supply the water, and a septic tank and leach field take the waste. When the pressure drops, the cause may be the pump, the tank's air charge, a clogged filter or a leak in the line, and each one has a different fix.

A septic system is also different from a town sewer. Slow drains and backups can mean a full tank, a clogged outlet baffle or a failing field, and the right response depends on which. Tell dispatch whether you are on a well, on septic or on public service.

Before we arrive in Somers: leak, pump failure or backup

With a well, shutting off the water at the main is not enough, because the pump keeps trying to supply the leak. That is why the pump breaker is part of the first step.

Follow these steps in order, then call.

  1. Switch off the well pump breaker, then close the main water shutoff.
  2. Open the lowest faucet to drain the lines and relieve pressure.
  3. If drains are backing up, stop using water entirely to protect the septic system.
  4. Move belongings off wet floors and photograph the damage.
  5. Call (860) 397-8103 and tell dispatch whether you have a well, septic or both.

A rural property needs a plan, not a guess. Dispatch is open and ready to ask the right questions.

Winter on a country road

Winter is hard on a rural system. A well house or pump line that is not insulated freezes, a lateral buried too shallow can freeze, and pipes in a house with a lowered thermostat split. A pump that cycles constantly in the cold may be fighting a frozen or leaking line.

If you spend part of the winter away, a neighbor with a key and a minimum heat setting are your best protection. See burst pipe repair for what to do when a pipe has already failed.
