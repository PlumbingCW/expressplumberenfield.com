Overflowing toilet: stop the water first

A toilet that is running over puts wastewater on your floor and down through the ceiling below. The fastest fix is cutting the supply, because the tank keeps refilling the bowl.

Follow these steps and then call.

  1. Turn the shutoff valve behind the toilet clockwise until it stops.
  2. Lift the tank lid and press the flapper down if the water is still rising.
  3. Keep children and pets out of the bathroom and wash hands after cleanup.
  4. If other drains gurgle or back up, stop using water in the house.
  5. Call (860) 397-8103 and say whether the problem is the toilet alone.

Common toilet problems and what they mean

A running toilet is usually a worn flapper, a fill valve that no longer shuts off or a chain that is too tight. A toilet that flushes weakly may have clogged rim jets or a partial blockage. A base that rocks or leaks at the floor often means the wax ring has failed or the flange is cracked or sitting too low.

A leak at the floor is worth acting on quickly because water seeps into the subfloor and rots it over time. When a toilet clogs repeatedly, the issue may be the drain behind it, and drain cleaning is the next step.

A toilet that overflows once may be a clog. One that overflows with other drains gurgling is a main-line problem.

Installing a new toilet

Replacing a toilet is a small job with a few details that matter. The flange has to be sound, the new wax ring or sealing gasket seated and the water supply line in good condition. We measure the rough-in distance from the wall to the flange bolts so the new toilet fits, and we remove the old one for disposal.

If the floor around the old toilet is soft, we show you before we set the new one, because a toilet on a rotten subfloor will rock again.

Why code matters for a toilet

Toilets, wax seals and supply stops are covered by the 2021 International Plumbing Code (IPC), which the 2022 Connecticut State Building Code adopts, including fixture clearances. Simple internal repairs rarely need a permit, while moving or replacing the drain does. The Connecticut Department of Consumer Protection licenses plumbers, so you can ask any plumber about their license before work starts.
