Failed water heater tank: what to do first

When the tank itself leaks, the water does not stop on its own, and a full tank holds a lot of it. The first ten minutes are about limiting the flood.

Do these in order and then call.

  1. Close the cold water supply valve on the pipe above the heater.
  2. Switch off the breaker for an electric heater, or turn a gas heater's control to off.
  3. Open a hot faucet upstairs to let air into the lines so the tank drains faster.
  4. If the floor is wet around an electrical panel, stay out and tell dispatch.
  5. Call (860) 397-8103 and say the fuel, the tank size and where the unit sits.

Replace or repair: how we decide

A tank that leaks from its seams is finished. So is one that has rusted through at the bottom or shows rust-colored water at every tap. A tank with a failed element or thermostat and no signs of rust may be worth a repair, and we say so even though a repair is a smaller job. The age plate and the condition of the shell tell most of the story.

Typical tank life is a decade or more, depending on use and water, so a heater that failed after ten years was not defective. It was finished.

A tank that has already failed is the easiest decision. Call and we will plan the swap while you stop the water.

What a replacement visit includes

We shut off and drain the old tank, disconnect the fuel, water and venting, and haul the old unit away. The new tank goes in with new flexible connectors, a new relief valve and discharge pipe, and a check of the venting or electrical supply. We fill it, purge the air, test for leaks and light or power it up.

If the system has no expansion tank and the house has a closed supply, we tell you why one is recommended. Rules for permits and venting vary, so we confirm what the Enfield building office needs for your unit. Planned work is covered under water heater installation.

Permits and code for replacement

Replacing a water heater is code work under the 2021 International Plumbing Code (IPC), which the 2022 Connecticut State Building Code adopts, and many towns expect a permit for it. Whether a permit is needed depends on the job and the town, and the Enfield building department can confirm it. Gas and flue connections are part of that code review.

Replacing tanks in Enfield homes

Enfield has newer subdivisions with attached garages and older sections where tanks sit in stone or poured-concrete basements. The old ones often have undersized or rusted vent connections that have to be corrected, not just reused, and we check the chimney or vent path every time.
