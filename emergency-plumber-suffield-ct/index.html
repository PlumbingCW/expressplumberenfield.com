Older homes, mixed water and sewer, and what that means for plumbing

Suffield has a long-settled town center and many older houses around it, and houses of that age carry plumbing from several eras. Supply lines may be copper, galvanized steel or newer plastic, and a house can have all three. The weak point is usually where two materials meet, which is where leaks and pressure loss start.

Not every Suffield home sits on the same water and sewer service. PURA's list of Connecticut water utilities shows Connecticut Water and the West Service Water Company serving the town, and some properties rely on private wells and septic systems instead of public service. Tell dispatch which one you have, because a well pump and a septic tank change the way we diagnose a problem.

Before we arrive in Suffield: burst pipe or active leak

A fast, calm first ten minutes limits the damage in an older house with finished walls and plaster ceilings. Water that runs behind a wall can show up two rooms away.

Do these steps in order, then call.

  1. Close the main water shutoff. On a well system, switch off the pump breaker too.
  2. Open the lowest faucet to drain the lines and take pressure off the break.
  3. Switch off power to wet rooms only if you can reach the panel while standing on dry ground.
  4. Clear furniture and rugs from the wet area and photograph the damage.
  5. Call (860) 397-8103 and tell dispatch whether you have town water or a well.

Winter in Suffield: why pipes freeze first in the outbuildings

Cold weather finds the weak spots in older houses. Pipes in exterior walls, over unheated garages and in barns or workshops with a spigot freeze first. Many Suffield properties have outbuildings with water, and a frozen line out there goes unnoticed until it thaws and pours.

If you are away for the winter, leave the heat on at a steady setting and open cabinet doors on exterior walls. A frozen line that has burst does not stay quiet, and a call to dispatch beats a trip back to a flooded house.
