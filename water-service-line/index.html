Emergency Water Line Repair in Enfield, CT

Water service line repair in Enfield, CT fixes the buried pipe that carries water from the utility main at the street into your house. When that pipe leaks or breaks, you may lose pressure, see a wet or sunken patch in the yard, or watch the meter spin, and we dispatch the emergency 24/7.

If water is surfacing in the yard, close the main shutoff inside the house, and tell dispatch whether the street valve or meter is involved.

Symptoms of a failing water service line

The first clue is often pressure. Showers weaken, faucets sputter and the dishwasher fills slowly, even though the problem is not inside the house. Other signs include a spongy or unusually green patch in the yard, the sound of running water near the meter, a bill that climbs and a meter that spins with every tap closed.

Low pressure has several causes, so we test pressure at an outdoor spigot and compare it with the pressure at the meter. That separates a leak on the service line from a clogged aerator or a failing pressure regulator in the house.

Water main break at the house: an emergency

A broken service line can flood a yard, undermine a driveway or push water into a foundation. If water is running and you cannot stop it inside the house, the problem is between the shutoff and the street.

Follow these steps while we head to you.

  1. Close the main water shutoff inside the house. If water continues to run in the yard, the leak is before your valve.
  2. Do not dig to find the pipe. Connecticut requires notice to 811 at least two full business days before digging with mechanized equipment.
  3. Keep children and pets away from soft or sunken ground near the line.
  4. Photograph the wet area and the meter reading.
  5. Call (860) 397-8103 and say whether the shutoff stopped the water.

A break in the service line can run for days before it shows. Call at the first sign of a wet yard or low pressure.

How we find and repair the break

We pressure-test the line, listen with ground microphones and use a locator to trace the pipe's path. Once we mark the break, we explain whether a small excavation and a coupling repair will do or whether the pipe is worn along its length and should be replaced. Copper, galvanized steel, polyethylene and older lead service lines all occur in New England, and the material changes both repair and cost.

Replacement runs a new line from the curb connection to the house, usually in one trench. Where trenching is difficult, we discuss alternatives. For a house with a long history of leaks, ask about repiping the interior lines at the same visit.

Rules for the water service line

The service line from the street to your meter and into the house is regulated plumbing work under the 2021 International Plumbing Code (IPC), which the 2022 Connecticut State Building Code adopts. Digging requires a call to 811 first, and the water company controls the connection at the main. Whether a permit is needed depends on the job and the town, and the Enfield building department can confirm it.

Who supplies water to Enfield homes

Connecticut's utility regulator lists Connecticut Water and Hazardville Water as the water suppliers for Enfield. Hazardville Water Company also serves parts of Somers, and public drinking water data lists its source as groundwater. Your bill tells you which one serves your address.

The meter and curb valve belong to the supplier, so a break near them needs the utility's crew as well. We coordinate with them. We serve the same shared systems in Somers and East Windsor.
