A sewer camera inspection in Enfield, CT sends a waterproof camera through your drain line so you can see pipe material, joints, sags, cracks and root entry on video. What a sewer camera inspection shows

The camera head is about the size of a small flashlight and carries its own lights. It is pushed through a cleanout toward the street while a monitor shows the pipe wall. We note the material, any offset joints, low spots where water pools, cracks, and where roots enter, then record the footage.

A locator on the camera lets us mark the surface directly above any defect, which turns a vague finding into a measured one. That is what lets a repair estimate be accurate instead of padded for uncertainty.

Good reasons to schedule an inspection

Most inspections fall into one of these situations.

  • <strong>Buying a house.</strong> A lateral that looks fine from the basement may be rooted or bellied near the street.
  • <strong>Repeat clogs.</strong> The same drain backing up within weeks usually points to pipe condition.
  • <strong>After a repair.</strong> Footage confirms that a spot repair or lining sits where it should.
  • <strong>Before landscaping.</strong> New trees and driveways are safer once you know where the line runs.
  • <strong>Older neighborhoods.</strong> Houses in Thompsonville and Hazardville can have laterals of different ages, so we confirm the line before quoting a repair.

Booking an inspection before closing is cheaper than finding a collapsed lateral after it.

What the camera can and cannot tell you

An inspection is powerful and still has limits worth knowing.

QuestionThe camera can answerLimit
What is the pipe made of?Yes, from joints and wall textureMixed materials need closer review
Is there a crack or offset?Yes, with a depth mark from the locatorA very dirty line may need cleaning first
Are roots present?Yes, with location and densityIt cannot say how fast they regrow
Is there a sag?Often, where water poolsA small belly may need a second pass
Is the street main sound?Only the part we can reachThe public main is the town's responsibility

Before, during and after the visit

Before the visit, tell us where the cleanout is, or let us find it. If there is none, we can usually enter through a toilet flange or a roof vent, though a cleanout installed first makes every later service easier. During the visit, you can watch the monitor and ask us to stop on anything that looks odd.

Afterward you get the findings in plain language and a recommendation. If the line is sound, we say so and leave it alone. If it is not, the next step is usually sewer line cleaning or sewer line repair, and you choose with the footage in hand.

What a camera inspection does and does not cover

A camera inspection records pipe condition but does not replace a code inspection. Any repair it leads to follows the 2021 International Plumbing Code (IPC), which the 2022 Connecticut State Building Code adopts. The Connecticut Department of Consumer Protection licenses plumbers, so you can ask any plumber about their license before work starts.

Inspecting laterals across Enfield and nearby towns

Enfield is a large town with distinct sections: Enfield Village, Thompsonville, Hazardville, Scitico and Sherwood Village. Lateral age and material can differ across them, so we avoid assuming that one street's pipe matches the next.
