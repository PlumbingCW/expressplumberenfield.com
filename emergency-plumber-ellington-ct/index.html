Newer homes, different problems

Ellington grew as a suburban town, and a large share of its housing is newer than the older village centers to the west. Newer houses are built with PEX or copper, finished basements and attached garages, and the failures differ. Supply lines and connectors wear, fixtures need replacing and water heaters reach the end of their life together because they were installed at the same time.

That pattern makes planned work a bigger share of the calls here than in the older river towns. We still treat leaks and failed tanks as emergencies, and we book the rest quickly.

Before we arrive in Ellington: burst pipe or active leak

In a finished house, a leak behind drywall can spread under flooring before anyone sees it. The first minutes are about finding and closing the valve.

Do these steps, then call.

  1. Close the main water shutoff. It is usually near the meter or where the line enters the basement.
  2. Open the lowest faucet to drain the lines.
  3. Switch off power to wet rooms only if you can reach the panel on dry footing.
  4. Photograph the damage and move belongings out of the way.
  5. Call (860) 397-8103 and tell dispatch where the water appears.

Cold weather and the long runs in a suburban house

Suburban houses have long pipe runs through garages, over-garage rooms and exterior walls. A pipe that freezes in a cold snap may not burst until it thaws, so a house can seem fine at breakfast and flood by afternoon.

If you see frost on a pipe or a faucet that runs slow in the cold, call before you try to thaw it with a heat source. Our burst pipe repair page explains what we do.

Codes that apply in Ellington

Ellington is governed by the same Connecticut code, which adopts the 2021 International Plumbing Code (IPC), which the 2022 Connecticut State Building Code adopts. Whether a permit is needed depends on the job and the town, and the Ellington building department can confirm it. The Connecticut Department of Consumer Protection licenses plumbers, so you can ask any plumber about their license before work starts.

Planned upgrades worth doing before they fail

Many Ellington homes have water heaters that were installed together and will fail within a few years of each other. Replacing yours before it leaks costs less than cleaning up after it. See water heater installation for planned work.

If you are remodeling a kitchen or finishing a basement, ask us to check shutoff valves, supply lines and drain routing before the walls close.

What to have ready when you call

A short list speeds up any call. Know your street address, where the main shutoff is, whether the problem is hot or cold water and roughly how long it has been running. If a fixture is leaking, tell us which room, and if a drain is backing up, tell us which fixtures are affected and whether the others still work.

For a rental or a house you manage, have the owner's contact and the access plan ready, because that decides how soon a crew can start. Photos help too, especially for hidden leaks and a stained ceiling, and they are useful later if you file an insurance claim.
