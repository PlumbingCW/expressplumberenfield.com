Sump pump failure: when a basement is taking on water

A sump pit that is full and a pump that is silent, or one that runs without moving water, means the basement will flood. Every minute matters, and the right first move depends on whether you can still reach the electric panel safely.

Take these steps while we are on the way.

  1. Check that the pump is plugged in and the breaker or GFCI has not tripped.
  2. Lift the float by hand, and if the pump runs, the float is the problem.
  3. Move boxes, rugs and electronics off the floor and away from the water.
  4. Stay out of standing water if outlets or the panel are wet.
  5. Call (860) 397-8103 and say whether the pump hums, runs or does nothing.

Why sump pumps fail

The most common causes are a stuck float switch, a failed motor, a clogged intake screen, a check valve that has stopped working and a power outage during the storm that overloaded the grid. Pumps also wear out from age. A pump that cycles constantly or runs for hours may be undersized for the water coming in.

A battery backup keeps the pump running when the power goes out, and a water-powered backup is another option where the supply allows. We discuss which fits your basement and your risk.

Installing a sump system

A new system starts with the pit. A sump basin collects water from the perimeter drain or from the slab, a pump lifts it and a discharge pipe carries it away from the house. The discharge has to drain well away from the foundation, or the water you pump out returns to the pit.

We choose the pump size from the basin and the water volume, install a check valve and a sealed lid, and test the system with water. Where the water problem is bigger than one pump can handle, basement waterproofing looks at the source.

Code and electrical notes for sump pumps

Sump pump discharge and piping follow the 2021 International Plumbing Code (IPC), which the 2022 Connecticut State Building Code adopts, and the electrical supply follows the electrical code. Use a properly grounded outlet and never run a cord through standing water. The Connecticut Department of Consumer Protection licenses plumbers, so you can ask any plumber about their license before work starts.
