When a faucet or valve problem is an emergency

Most faucet problems are a nuisance, not a crisis. A shutoff valve that will not close, a supply line that is spraying or a shower valve that leaks into the wall are different.

Take these steps while we head to you.

  1. Close the angle stop under the sink or behind the fixture.
  2. If it will not close, shut the main water valve for the house.
  3. Turn off power to any outlet that water has reached, only if the panel is dry.
  4. Place a bucket, move belongings and photograph the leak.
  5. Call (860) 397-8103 and say which fixture is leaking and whether the valve moved.

Why faucets leak and valves fail

Inside a faucet, rubber seals, washers and cartridges wear. Water then escapes past them, which shows as a drip at the spout or a leak around the handle. Hard use and mineral scale hasten the wear. Cartridge and ceramic disc designs are replaceable, and often the repair is a new cartridge, not a new faucet.

Shutoff valves under a sink often sit unused for years and then seize or leak when someone finally turns them. A valve that is stuck is not a safe thing to force. We replace it so you have a working shutoff before the next problem.

A faucet that will not stop leaking can be an hour from a big mess. Call and we will tell you how to hold it.

Shower and tub valves

A shower valve sits inside the wall, so a leak can be hidden. If the shower will not shut off fully, or water appears on the other side of the wall, the cartridge or valve body may be failing. Many cartridges can be replaced from the front, but a valve body with a cracked or corroded joint needs access through the wall.

We tell you before we open anything and show you what we find. The same approach applies to a tub spout that diverts poorly or a hand shower that leaks behind the plate.

Standards for fixtures and supply lines

Faucet replacement and supply line work follow the 2021 International Plumbing Code (IPC), which the 2022 Connecticut State Building Code adopts, including approved fittings and shutoff valves. Whether a permit is needed depends on the job and the town, and the Enfield building department can confirm it.
