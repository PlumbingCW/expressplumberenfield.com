How to Find Your Main Water Shutoff in Enfield, CT Before You Need It

The main water shutoff is the valve that stops all water entering your house. When a pipe bursts or a fixture fails, closing it is the fastest way to limit the damage. Most people do not know where theirs is until they need it, and that is the worst time to look.

This guide explains where to find it, how to test it, what to do if it is stuck and how to handle the second valve at the street.

Where the main shutoff usually is

In most houses, the water line enters through the foundation wall or floor, and the shutoff is a few feet from that point. In a basement, look near the front foundation wall, close to the water meter, in a utility area or near the water heater. In a house on a slab, it may be in a closet, a garage or a mechanical room.

The line enters on the side of the house facing the street in most cases, so start there. If you have a well, the main shutoff is near the pressure tank, and the pump breaker is a second control you should know.

How to test your shutoff

01

Find the valve

Look at the pipe where the water enters and find the first valve after the meter. Tag it with a bright label so anyone in the house can find it.

02

Open a faucet

Run a tap at the lowest point in the house so you can see the water stop.

03

Close the valve slowly

Turn it all the way. A gate valve may take several turns. Do not force it if it resists.

04

Check the tap

The flow should slow and stop within a short time. A drip for a few seconds is normal, and a steady stream means the valve is not sealing.

05

Reopen and note the result

Open it again, and run a tap to clear air. Write down whether it closed fully and how many turns it took.

If the valve is stuck or leaks

A valve that has not moved in years can seize. Do not use a wrench to force a gate valve, because the stem can snap and the valve can fail in the open position. A valve that leaks when turned can be tightened at the packing nut a small amount, but one that drips constantly needs replacement.

If you cannot close the valve during a leak, the next valve is at the street, in a curb box, and it needs a key. Call your water supplier, and call a plumber at the same time. We can also replace an old gate valve with a quarter-turn ball valve, so it closes in a second.

Other shutoffs worth finding today

A leak at one fixture does not need the whole house off.

  • <strong>Fixture stops.</strong> Small valves under sinks and behind toilets close water to one fixture.
  • <strong>Water heater valve.</strong> A cold supply valve on top of the tank isolates it.
  • <strong>Washing machine valves.</strong> Hot and cold valves behind the washer.
  • <strong>Outdoor spigot valve.</strong> An inside valve to shut off and drain outdoor lines.
  • <strong>Irrigation valve.</strong> A separate shutoff for a sprinkler system.

What to do in a leak, step by step

If water is escaping, close the main valve first. Then open the lowest faucet to drain the lines, which lowers pressure and slows the leak. Move belongings away from the water, and switch off power to wet areas only if you can reach the panel on dry ground.

Then call a plumber. Tell us where the water is, whether the valve closed and what you see. A burst pipe repair visit stops the leak and replaces the failed section, and a leak detection visit finds one you cannot see.

Teach everyone in the house

A shutoff is only useful if someone can reach it. Show every adult and older child where it is and how it turns. Keep a flashlight near it, and keep the area around it clear. If you rent the house, ask the landlord to show you and tag the valve.

If you travel often or have a vacation home, consider shutting off the main when you leave, especially in winter. Leave the heat on, drain the lines if the house will be empty for long and have someone check on it.

When to replace an old shutoff valve

Replace a valve that will not close fully, weeps at the stem or has a corroded body. Replacement is also wise before a remodel, because a good shutoff lets the plumber work without calling the utility. A new quarter-turn ball valve closes quickly, and you can see at a glance whether it is open or closed.

A quick family drill

Practice once. Pick a time when everyone is home, and have each person find the valve and close it while another person watches a faucet. Time how long it takes. A drill like this turns a panic into a routine, and it exposes a stuck valve before a leak does.

Take a photo of the valve with its label, and keep it in your phone. Write the supplier's phone number on a card beside it, and add our number, (860) 397-8103, for a plumbing emergency. Keep the card where a guest or a house sitter will see it too.

Code background on shutoff valves

Shutoff valves are required equipment under the 2021 International Plumbing Code (IPC), which the 2022 Connecticut State Building Code adopts, and replacing a failed main valve is licensed plumbing work. Whether a permit is needed depends on the job and the town, and the Enfield building department can confirm it.

The valve at the street and your supplier in Enfield

PURA's list of Connecticut water utilities shows Connecticut Water and Hazardville Water serving Enfield. Your bill shows which one serves your address, and that supplier owns the meter and the curb valve. Do not operate the curb valve yourself, and ask your supplier where its responsibility ends at your property.
