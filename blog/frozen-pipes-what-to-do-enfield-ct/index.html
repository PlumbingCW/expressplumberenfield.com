Frozen Pipes in Enfield, CT: What to Do Before and After They Freeze

A frozen pipe is a water line with ice in it. The ice blocks the flow, and as it expands it can split the pipe. The split often shows only when the ice thaws and water pours out, which is why a house can look fine in the morning and flood in the afternoon.

This guide explains how to tell a pipe is frozen, how to thaw it safely, what to do if it has split and how to keep it from happening again.

If a pipe has already burst

Water coming out of a split pipe can release a lot in a short time. Stopping it comes before cleanup, and the main shutoff is the key.

Follow these steps in order.

  1. Close the main water shutoff.
  2. Open the lowest faucet to drain the lines.
  3. Switch off power to wet areas only if you can reach the panel on dry footing.
  4. Move belongings off wet floors and photograph the damage for your insurer.
  5. Call (860) 397-8103 and describe where the water is coming from.

Signs that a pipe is frozen

The clearest sign is a faucet that runs slowly or not at all in cold weather, when other taps work. Frost on a visible pipe, a pipe that is cold and stiff to the touch and a bulge in the pipe are other signs. Sometimes you hear a faint hissing or see an odd smell from a drain.

Pipes freeze first where cold air reaches them. Exterior walls, unheated basements and crawlspaces, garages, attics and the spaces behind kitchen cabinets on outside walls are the usual spots.

A frozen pipe is a warning. A burst pipe is an emergency, and our line answers 24/7.

How to thaw a frozen pipe safely

01

Open the faucet

Leave the tap open so water can flow as the ice melts, and so you see when flow returns. Melting ice also relieves pressure in the line.

02

Find the frozen section

Feel along the pipe for the cold or frosted spot. Start at the faucet side and work back toward the source.

03

Apply gentle heat

Use a hair dryer, a heating pad or warm towels. Move the heat along the pipe, working toward the frozen part. Never use an open flame, a torch or a propane heater, which can start a fire.

04

Check for leaks as it thaws

Watch the pipe and the area around it. If water appears, close the main shutoff and call a plumber.

05

Keep the water moving

Once flow returns, leave the faucet running a trickle for a while, and check other taps. If one pipe froze, others may be close behind.

Prevention before the cold snap

A few steps cut the risk. Disconnect garden hoses and shut off the inside valve to outdoor spigots, and drain them. Insulate pipes in unheated spaces with foam sleeves. Seal gaps where cold air enters near pipes, and open cabinet doors on exterior walls during a cold night so warm air reaches the pipes.

Keep the thermostat at a steady setting, day and night, and do not drop it far when you leave. Letting a faucet drip on a very cold night keeps water moving. If you will be away, arrange for someone to check the house, because a burst in an empty house runs until someone finds it.

After the thaw: repair and a hard look at why it froze

If a pipe split, a plumber cuts out the damaged section and replaces it with new pipe, then checks the rest of the run for weakness. We also look at why it froze. A pipe that froze once in a given spot will do it again unless the cause is fixed, whether that is a missing piece of insulation, a gap or a line that needs rerouting.

For a full description of the repair, see burst pipe repair. If the house has repeated freezing problems or old galvanized lines, a repiping plan is worth pricing.

A cold-night checklist for the whole house

On the coldest nights, a few minutes of attention pays for itself. Open the cabinet doors under kitchen and bathroom sinks on exterior walls. Let a faucet drip on the line farthest from the main shutoff. Check that garage doors are closed, that the basement windows are shut and that no vents are blowing cold air at a pipe.

If a storm may knock out power, know where your main shutoff is and how to close it. If your heat fails, call for service early and keep the house as warm as you can. A house that loses heat in a hard freeze can have pipes splitting within a day.

Insulation choices and what they do

Foam pipe sleeves are cheap and easy to fit on exposed pipes in unheated spaces. Fiberglass wraps work too, but they need a moisture cover. Heat tape, also called heat cable, is another option for a stubborn spot, and it must be installed as the instructions say, because misuse is a fire risk.

Insulation slows heat loss but does not make heat. A pipe in a space with no warmth at all can still freeze in a long cold snap. The best protection is warm air near the pipe, so keep the heat on and seal the gaps that let cold air in.

Thawing safety and code notes

Never use an open flame to thaw a pipe, because it can ignite framing and damage the pipe. Permanent repairs after a split follow the 2021 International Plumbing Code (IPC), which the 2022 Connecticut State Building Code adopts. The Connecticut Department of Consumer Protection licenses plumbers, so you can ask any plumber about their license before work starts.
