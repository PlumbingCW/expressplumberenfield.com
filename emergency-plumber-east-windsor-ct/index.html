Two villages, two housing stocks

East Windsor is a town of distinct villages. Warehouse Point sits along the river and has older homes and a long history tied to the water. The Connecticut Trolley Museum is there, a landmark that tells you the age of the neighborhood. Broad Brook is a separate village to the east with its own ZIP code and its own older housing.

Both areas have houses with plumbing from different decades, so we expect mixed pipe materials, older shutoff valves and basements with water heaters that have been working for a long time.

Before we arrive in East Windsor: failed water heater or flooding

A water heater that has failed can put a lot of water on a basement floor quickly, and a house near the river may already have damp ground. Start with the water, then the power.

Take these steps while the crew is on the way.

  1. Shut the cold supply valve above the water heater.
  2. Turn an electric heater off at the breaker, or switch a gas unit to off.
  3. Clear the floor, move electronics and photograph the damage.
  4. If water is rising from the floor drain, stop all water use.
  5. Call (860) 397-8103 and tell dispatch the fuel type and where the water is coming from.

East Windsor is a short run south of us. Call now and we start planning while you shut off the water.

Water heaters, leaks and the basements near the river

Water heater failures lead the calls we expect from older neighborhoods, because tanks in basements that have been in place for years are more likely to leak than to need a part. See our pages on water heater repair and water heater replacement.

Basements near the river and its brooks also take on water after heavy rain, and a sump pump that fails in a storm is an emergency. We repair and install pumps and look at the source of the water.

Spring thaw and heavy rain in a river town

Seasonal water changes the problem list. Spring thaw and long rain spells raise groundwater, and low basements are the first to feel it. A pump that has sat unused all winter may fail the first time it runs, so we recommend a test pour before the wet season.

In the cold months, pipes in exterior walls and unheated spaces freeze. A burst in an older house can run a long time before someone notices. See burst pipe repair for what we do.

Codes that apply in East Windsor

East Windsor uses the same state code, which adopts the 2021 International Plumbing Code (IPC), which the 2022 Connecticut State Building Code adopts. Whether a permit is needed depends on the job and the town, and the East Windsor building department can confirm it. The Connecticut Department of Consumer Protection licenses plumbers, so you can ask any plumber about their license before work starts.

Water supply and the towns around East Windsor

PURA's list of Connecticut water utilities shows Connecticut Water and the MDC serving East Windsor, and Hazardville Water Company's service area includes East Windsor and parts of Enfield and Somers. Your bill shows your supplier, and we use that to decide where a service line problem begins.

East Windsor borders Enfield, so we reach it along I-91 and Route 5. We also serve Ellington to the east and Windsor Locks across the river.

What we ask on the first call from East Windsor

Dispatch asks which village you are in, whether the building is a house, a rental or a business and what the water is doing right now. Those three answers tell us how to route the crew and what to bring, whether that is a drain machine, a water heater or a pump.

If you are in Warehouse Point or Broad Brook and cannot reach the main shutoff, say so, and we talk you through where to look. A house near the river may have the shutoff in a utility corner of the basement, and finding it early saves floor and furniture.
